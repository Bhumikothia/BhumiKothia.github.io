## The problem, in plain language

Factories produce wastewater clouded with tiny suspended particles. They are too small and too light to sink on their own. To clean the water, treatment plants add a **flocculant**, a substance that makes the particles clump into larger "flocs" that settle to the bottom and can be removed.

Most plants use **chemical flocculants** such as aluminium or iron salts and synthetic polymers. They work, but they have drawbacks. They can leave chemical residues in treated water, they create large volumes of chemical-laden sludge, and some synthetic polymers break down slowly in the environment.

## A natural alternative

Some bacteria release sticky natural substances called **extracellular polymeric substances (EPS)**, made mostly of sugars and proteins. Some of these substances are powerful flocculants in their own right. These **bioflocculants** are biodegradable and come from microbes that can be grown on inexpensive nutrients. That makes them a promising, more sustainable way to help wastewater settle.

The hard parts are finding bacteria that produce good bioflocculants, producing enough of the material affordably, and understanding what it is made of. Bhumi's Ph.D. research tackles all three.

## What her research does

1. **Finds the right bacteria.** She isolates bacteria from real industrial and municipal waste streams and screens them on different growth media to find strong bioflocculant producers.
   Published in her [journal article](../publications/#2026-bbra-microbial-diversity-bioflocculant) and the dataset on [bioflocculant-producing bacteria from complex waste streams](../publications/#2026-data-bioflocculant-complex-waste-streams). She first presented this work at [IAES-02 (2023)](../talks/#2023-10-iaes-02).

2. **Identifies them.** She confirms each isolate's identity with 16S rRNA gene sequencing and bioinformatics, and characterises its shape and structure.
   Sequences are deposited in [NCBI GenBank](../publications/#data-deposits), with datasets on [16S rRNA characterisation](../publications/#2026-data-16s-rrna-four-isolates) and [molecular and morpho-structural characterisation](../publications/#2026-data-molecular-morpho-structural).

3. **Makes more flocculant.** She tests which carbon and nitrogen sources and growth conditions increase bioflocculant production.
   She presented this at [CVMU Research Week-24](../talks/#2024-01-cvmu-research-week), [CTBS-2024](../talks/#2024-02-ctbs-vii), [ICAEBIO-24](../talks/#2024-05-icaebio-24) and [ICMBT-2024](../talks/#2024-08-icmbt-2024), and published datasets on [optimised cultivation parameters](../publications/#2026-data-optimized-bioflocculants) and [flocculating activity](../publications/#2026-data-flocculating-activity-bacillus).

4. **Characterises the flocculant.** She studies the soluble EPS that the bacteria release into their growth medium, to understand how it binds particles together.

## Why it matters

Industrial wastewater treatment is a large, ongoing cost for manufacturers and a major route by which chemicals enter the environment. Bioflocculants that work reliably at scale could help plants:

- reduce their reliance on synthetic and metal-based chemical flocculants,
- produce sludge that is easier to handle and less chemically loaded, and
- move toward treatment processes built on renewable, biodegradable materials.

Her background in industrial environmental health and safety work keeps the research grounded in what treatment plants and compliance teams actually need.

## Technical summary

*For specialists.*

- **Organisms:** bioflocculant-producing bacteria isolated from industrial and municipal waste streams. The deposited 16S rRNA sequences are from *Bacillus* species (*B. paranthracis*, *B. halotolerans*, *B. velezensis*, *B. thuringiensis*).
- **Product:** soluble extracellular polymeric substances recovered from cell-free culture supernatant.
- **Methods:** selective isolation and media-specific screening; flocculating-activity assays; optimisation of carbon and nitrogen sources and cultivation parameters; 16S rRNA gene sequencing with a BLAST, EzBioCloud and VSEARCH bioinformatics pipeline and phylogenetic placement; morphological and structural characterisation; EPS characterisation.
- **Open data:** five datasets on Mendeley Data (CC BY 4.0) and four GenBank accessions. The full list is on the [Publications & Datasets](../publications/) page.
